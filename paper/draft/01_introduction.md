# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Context and motivation

Agent skills are portable directories that combine structured metadata, natural-language instructions, and optional scripts and reference materials. At their core, a `SKILL.md` file specifies how an LLM-based agent should perform a task, including the constraints it must follow and the resources it may use \cite{agentskills_spec}. Although software reuse has been extensively studied \cite{haefliger2008code,lopes2017dejavu}, agent skills introduce distinct challenges because their distribution and reuse do not depend on a centralized registry or package management system. Instead, skills can be replicated by copying their directories across repositories. However, the presence of identical skill content in multiple repositories does not necessarily indicate independent adoption. Such replication may reflect characteristics of the skill itself, such as clarity and portability, or external factors, including its inclusion in templates or catalogs, automated installation by development tools, and duplication across projects maintained by the same account. Consequently, observed replication patterns may arise from both intrinsic characteristics of the skill and the context in which it is distributed, making it difficult to determine what drives reuse.

The GitSkills dataset provides an opportunity to investigate these patterns at scale by documenting skill occurrences, distinct contents, and associated repository metadata \cite{gitskills2027}. Previous research has examined defects in skill specifications \cite{zhang2026keepskillsreuse}, the effectiveness of skills in software engineering tasks \cite{han2026sweskillsbench}, and the evolution of prompts in software repositories \cite{tafreshipour2025prompting}. Building on this literature, the present study investigates whether observable characteristics of agent skills and their repositories are associated with replication across distinct GitHub owners. Importantly, it also examines whether the apparent predictability of such replication is influenced by the procedures used to construct the dataset, which may introduce systematic biases into the observed relationships.

## 1.2 Research problem

We operationalize reuse as the presence of identical `SKILL.md` content (same `content_hash`) in distinct repositories. The natural target, the number of repositories that contain a content (`repository_count`), is fragile for two reasons. First, GitSkills keeps detailed metadata for only one **representative** copy of each content, chosen by the best location class (preferring `.claude/skills/`) and then, as we recovered, alphabetically by repository and path; since a content with more copies is more likely to have some copy in the preferred folder, the representative's location class, and the commit-history indicator that depends on it, carry information about the target itself. Second, about a quarter of the reused skills (24.7% in the full population, 28.0% in the development set) have all copies under a **single owner**, and one account alone accounts for 12% of the development set, so counting repositories mixes diffusion among developers with duplication by one account.

We therefore define the target in terms of owners. The main target is **cross-owner reuse** (`cross_owner`: the content appears under two or more owners) and, secondarily, the number of owners (`owner_count`); the attributes exclude the variables that depend on the representative rule, which are analyzed as a finding. The evaluation uses owner-grouped validation, a per-owner cap, nested tuning and pre-specified hypotheses, and confirmation is done on a **new holdout, disjoint by owner**, evaluated once. Because the protocol was fixed after an exploratory phase, we distinguish what is confirmatory from what is exploratory. Conclusions are observational associations \cite{shmueli2010explain}: we claim neither causality nor skill quality.

**Target population.** The study conditions on contents observed in at least two repositories (`repository_count >= 2`). It distinguishes cross-owner replication from within-owner duplication; it does not predict initial reuse among all skills, nor whether an arbitrary new skill will ever be reused. This conditioning also explains why cross-owner reuse is the majority class (about 80%).

**Scope of the prediction.** This study evaluates whether observable attributes can discriminate between skills with different levels of *observed* cross-owner replication in a fixed repository snapshot. It does not evaluate the prospective prediction of future reuse: attributes and target come from the same July 2026 snapshot, so a repository's stars, forks and last-push date may have been measured after some or all of the copying had already happened. The problem is cross-sectional, not prospective.

## 1.3 Objectives and research questions

* **RQ1 — Construction biases:** to what extent do the representative-selection rule, self-copying and owner concentration confound the target and the attributes of GitSkills?
* **RQ2 — Predictability:** after excluding the identified construction-dependent shortcuts, can cross-owner reuse and the number of owners be predicted better than baselines, and is the estimate confirmed on an owner-disjoint holdout?
* **RQ3 — Origin of the signal:** what is the contribution of skill, repository and catalog attributes, which variables are associated with the predictions, and in which direction?

## 1.4 Contributions

(i) An **audit of the GitSkills representative**: we recover its rule (97.2% agreement) and show that location and history depend on the number of copies. (ii) A **protocol** for cross-owner reuse with an owner-based target, a per-owner cap, grouped validation, pre-specified hypotheses and confirmation on a new holdout. (iii) Evidence of a **modest predictive signal**, with a stable RMSLE gain across protocol variations, confirmed on unseen data (AUC 0.73) and decomposed into blocks (an exploratory decomposition). (iv) A **reproducible package** with the data revision, seeds, a five-minute reproduction path and a lock that prevents reusing the holdout.
