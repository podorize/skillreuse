# Appendix A — Reproducibility

**Code, data and models.** All code, the dates collected through the GitHub API (already processed), the study table, the trained models and the result files are available at <https://github.com/podorize/skillreuse>. The data come from a fixed revision of GitSkills (`ebab17454a7c236f8f26b183567f1a126f42e3f8`), verified by SHA-256. The environment is Python 3.12 with every dependency pinned in `uv.lock`. All random choices use fixed seeds: `20261121` for the sample, `20261122` for the split, 2027–2029 for the cross-validation folds, 2027 for models, searches and bootstrap, and `20261123` for the interpretation sample.

**How to reproduce.** Start with the Colab notebook (<https://colab.research.google.com/github/podorize/skillreuse/blob/main/notebooks/reproduce.ipynb>): with no installation and in a few minutes, it follows this paper section by section and compares every number with the result files. Locally, after `uv sync`, there are four levels, one command each (times on a laptop with 8 GB of memory):

1. *Check the results* (`make verify`, under a minute): applies the trained models to the holdout and checks every number in this paper.
2. *Redo the analysis* (`make rerun`, about 20 minutes): repeats every step, from the exploratory analysis to the interpretation.
3. *Redo the processing* (`make rebuild`, about 30 minutes plus a 7 GB download): also rebuilds the sample and the study table from GitSkills.
4. *Redo from scratch* (`make from-scratch`, about 8 hours): also dates the copies again through the GitHub API; the result is close but not identical, as repositories have changed.

**Collection details.** Of the 223,261 copies outside aggregators, 88.9% were dated; the others were in repositories deleted or made private after the snapshot (9.4%), had lost the file (0.7%), failed after three retries (0.4%), had an arrival after the snapshot (0.3%) or no longer contained the exact content (0.2%). For 11.7% of the dated copies the first commit predated the repository (inherited history), and the arrival rule moved them to the repository's creation. Of the 84,995 copies in aggregators, collected for the robustness analysis, 86.3% were dated.

**Additional robustness checks.** Two checks complement Section 4.4, both on the holdout with the frozen text model. Restricting the holdout to the 19,862 skills whose copies could all be dated, since an undated copy could hide the true origin or a copy within the window, gives an AUC of 0.626 (95% CI [0.600; 0.649]); these skills are copied less often (5.5%), since skills with more copies more often lost one to a deleted repository. Restricting it to the 15,154 skills (73.7%) whose front-matter name never appears in the development set, to rule out the recognition of variants of known skills, gives 0.622 (95% CI [0.593; 0.651]).

**Safeguards.** The analysis plan was written down before any model was trained (`paper/protocol.md`). The final models were saved with their hashes before the holdout was read, and the holdout evaluation is locked after its single run. Four deviations were recorded in the plan: copies with an arrival after the snapshot are treated as undated; the copies in aggregators were collected later, in smaller batches, because large batches stalled; because the GitHub API no longer lists stargazers with their dates, the popularity of the origin repository at T0 was replaced, after the single evaluation, by its popularity at the snapshot (the original control is kept in `results/phase6/holdout_results.json`); and the planned ALE plots were not reported, since for a linear model they repeat the SHAP dependence plots.
