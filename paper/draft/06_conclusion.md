# 6. Conclusion and future work

This work investigated whether observable characteristics of a skill and of its repository are associated with cross-owner reuse in GitSkills, and how much of this predictability is an artifact of the dataset's construction. Three conclusions hold.

**First**, GitSkills has construction biases that affect predictive studies. The rule that selects the representative makes the probability of it being in `.claude/skills/` grow from 14% to 92% with the number of copies, while the actual share of copies in that folder stays at 5–13%. This makes location and commit history spurious predictors of reuse. In addition, 28% of reused skills exist under a single owner, and a few accounts concentrate the sample, so that counting repositories overstates diffusion and performance depends on a few owners (AUC from 0.615 to 0.747 depending on the treatment).

**Second**, free of these artifacts, the attributes predict cross-owner reuse with a modest signal, confirmed on new data: on the owner-disjoint holdout, the Random Forest reaches an AUC of 0.731 (95% CI 0.716–0.745) and the HistGradientBoosting reduces the RMSLE of `owner_count` by 6% relative to the mean (0.625 versus 0.664). Skill and repository contribute complementarily, and the signal is associated mainly with repository visibility (stars, forks), with older last pushes and, in the regression, with the absence of a license. At a decision threshold the model has a balanced accuracy of 0.66 and a precision of 0.28 for single-owner content, so it ranks rather than classifies.

**Third**, the limit seems to be in the data and not in the models: six families tie and tuning does not help. The claims are associative and do not identify what makes a skill more or less copied, nor its quality.

**Future work.** (i) Model at the copy level, with information prior to reuse, in order to use attributes of all copies without leakage. (ii) Group near-identical copies to mitigate the fragmentation of the target by exact hash. (iii) Replicate with later GitSkills snapshots and study the temporal dynamics of diffusion. (iv) Investigate why repositories without a license host skills with more owners, and the role of catalog size. (v) Evaluate text representations (for example, embeddings) within fixed location classes. (vi) Propose to the GitSkills maintainers indicators that separate the representative choice from the repository attributes.
